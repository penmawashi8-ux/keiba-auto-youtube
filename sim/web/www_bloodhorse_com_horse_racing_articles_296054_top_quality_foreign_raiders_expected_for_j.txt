Top-Quality Foreign Raiders Expected for Japan Cup
Report from Japan
By
Naohiro Goda
November 16, 2026
The 46th running of Japan Cup (G1), which carries total purse of ¥1.09 billion (approx. US$6.77 million) Nov. 29 at Tokyo Racecourse, is one of the most exciting renewals.
The Japan Cup was inaugurated in 1981 as the first international race in Japan, and the field for the second running in 1982 was full of stars from all over the world. Representing the United States was reigning Horse of the Year and future Hall of Famer John Henry; Irish-bred and French-trained April Run had won the Turf Classic (G1T) at Belmont Park in 1981 and 1982; All Along was winner of the Prix Vermeille (G1) and awarded as U.S. Horse of the Year in 1983, and Isle of Man, was the winner of New Zealand Derby (G1).
The quality of foreign raiders this year is at least similar to 1982, if not better.
Calandagan
, winner of the Japan Cup and the world's highest rated horse in 2025;
Ombudsman
, winner of five group 1s and the highest rated European-trained horse in training so far in 2026;
Minnie Hauk
, European champion 3-year-old filly in 2025;
Ethical Diamond
, winner of the Breeders' Cup Turf (G1T) in 2025;
Goliath
, winner of three group 1s; and
Teao
, Chilean Horse of the Year, are intended runners and have booked their flights to Japan, which arrive Nov. 19.
In addition to the purse, Ombudsman, winner of Champion Stakes (G1) in 2026, and Ethical Diamond are eligible for a US$5 million bonus.
